A tiny edit no longer recomputes the whole cache

The first candidate still receives a raw copy of the old atlas and node buffer. The builder skips proved-unaffected subtrees, initializes every newly exposed child block, preserves retained brick slots, and samples only affected bricks. New or numerically ambiguous samples keep canonical evaluation.

The stop gate: a queue cannot fix a service deficit

At one accepted stamp per 60 Hz frame, a four-stamp batch must free its slots within about four frames. A 2 ms/frame maintenance allowance provides at most 8 ms of GPU work in that interval. Guardian cuts exceed it even before preparation, scheduling and publication overhead. Eight pending slots only postpone admission pauses.

What to keep and what to investigate next

Keep the qualified building blocks

  • Local invalidation plus exposed-child closure.
  • Stable brick slots and bounded append space.
  • Raw candidate copy with the ordinary Cached layout.
  • Cheap eight-sphere preview and exact prefix accounting.

Resolve Guardian’s remaining source-evaluation cost first

  • Measure reuse or amortization of canonical node-center evaluations.
  • Compare a lower stamp rate or a larger explicit maintenance allowance.
  • Keep CPU source preparation off the frame thread and include it in job age.
  • Then qualify paced scheduling, reader retirement and real input admission.

The raw-copy floor is small enough that a shared atlas, journal or general allocator is not the first optimization justified by this study. Root dominance helps new-brick sampling, but leaves expensive source evaluation in classification and uncertain fallback. Changing that needs its own raw-cache qualification.
