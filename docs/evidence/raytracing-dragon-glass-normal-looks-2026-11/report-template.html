Where to look

The head grooves and broad neck/body refraction show the normal-policy change most clearly. The outline, pose and base geometry are identical. Use Blink to judge whether the local polish matters at the scene's normal display size.

Keep sampling noise separate

Seed B provides another front-view sample realization. The plain-base, sky-only pair removes marble pattern/bump and the small solar emitter in both modes. It is an isolation control; the other views retain the actual demo composition.

What changes and what stays matched

The experimental helper chooses a guarded interpolated normal for each sharp-glass interface, falling back as a whole to the geometric normal. The existing dielectric sampler and Fresnel/radiance weighting then use that selected normal. Reflection and refraction both change.

Both private builds use the same geometry, recipes, lighting, camera, exposure, RNG dimensions, sample count and path budget. Geometric entry/exit, medium tracking, ray spawning and sun-shadow approximation stay unchanged. The scalar footprint model is retained in both modes; varying-normal filtering is not qualified here.

The original executable, shaders, presets and model are preserved. These are separate source/build snapshots rather than a newly shipped setting.

Capture receipts and experimental limitations

This report answers an appearance question. The earlier interface controls found continuity and energy limitations in this approximation; those findings remain recorded in the Q1 numerical report. They are not substituted for visual judgment here.
