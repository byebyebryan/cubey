The sun is the main source of the unstable white pattern. With the marble unchanged, the sky-only head crop has zero pixels with all three RGB8 channels at least 240. At 4,096 samples, the combined seeds have 636 and 618 such pixels; only 179 overlap. Repeated white pixels alone do not prove convergence.

Three samples can dominate a white pixel

The orange-marked pixel, (818, 372), has average HDR luminance 16.25 with seed 19 and 0.320 with seed 43. In seed 19, three solar samples supply almost all its brightness. Each contributes about 21,750 luminance units after five or six surface events; the median sample is 0.245. The pixel's status flags are zero.

The green-marked highlight, (738, 527), stays near luminance 39 in both seeds. It also depends on rare bright samples: nine high-energy solar samples dominate seed 19. A broad clamp would therefore suppress repeatable glints as well as unstable estimates.

More samples improve HDR convergence, but the display still sparkles

Doubling 4,096 to 8,192 samples reduces the head region's HDR seed difference from 0.434 to 0.300, about 31%. Display-space seed difference rises from 7.72 to 8.83 RGB8 codes, and more pixels cross the white threshold as rare solar paths are discovered. The 8,192-sample images remain noisy comparisons, rather than ground truth.

The broader sun is a lighting choice

The private preview uses four times the shared atmosphere's solar angular radius: approximately 1.07° instead of 0.268°. The direct illumination strength stays fixed and the existing traced emitter normalizes its radiance by source area. Glass remains sharp. This lowers HDR variance but produces a denser glint pattern at the tested budget; it does not deliver an obvious visual cleanup.

A bounded next experiment

Separate the solar contribution at the end of glass paths and compare a small solar-only filter against an explicit reduction of solar specular strength. Keep the smooth sky refraction, marble lighting and shared atmosphere intact. The tradeoff is a calmer image versus loss or softening of true glints.

Judge the options at 128/512 samples, first at whole-scene size and then in the head crop, with independent seeds and a short native orbit. Keep the unfiltered path as a reference and change a default only after visual review. This proposal is not implemented.

Why ordinary light sampling or a denoiser is not a quick switch

Our sharp-glass branch follows exact reflected/refracted directions; explicit solar sampling is used for rough interfaces, while only the first two events split Fresnel branches. A small bright sun reached through a longer specular chain is difficult to sample. PBRT describes this solar/glass variance mechanism.

Cycles documents clamping as a noise/brightness tradeoff. NRD's guidance requires depth, normal and motion information and describes special handling around glass; a clear refractive dragon needs more than an opaque-surface denoiser toggle. Specular manifold sampling targets these difficult paths through a dedicated path-connection method. That is a larger rendering experiment.
