Regional pruning remains unqualified here

Regional reduction is useful, but its earlier point-level contract does not imply bit-identical sampled caches. A4 changes Guardian R16 values while preserving topology. A16 also changes Guardian's leaf sets, and changes Robot samples. Neither route is adopted.

Where the next substantial gain can come from
Reuse still visits every destination brick. Robot now spends most GPU build time copying samples and applying the tool across the cache. Guardian also pays to evaluate canonical source for new bricks and numerical fallbacks. Its study validation and full hierarchy classification remain material costs.
  1. Test conservative local work. Derive which hierarchy cells and sampled bricks can change under one hard root operation. Use dominance bounds with numerical margins; a raw brush AABB alone is insufficient.
  2. Investigate stable atlas slots. Retain unchanged bricks without globally remapping and rewriting them. Count allocation, reclamation, failure and rendering costs.
  3. Budget maintenance alongside rendering. Split classification, sampling and copying into measured units, retain one pending edit and publish coherent versions. Measure complete frame deadlines and service rate.
This loop stops at that decision. No persistent allocator, background updater, brush UI, undo UI, larger source capacity or XR qualification is implemented. The full canonical builder remains the recovery/control path.
