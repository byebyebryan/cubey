# Dragon glass normal appearance comparison

2026-11-14–15 local run. Follows the user's request to compare the actual
looks after the [Q1 interface experiment](raytracing-dragon-glass-q1-results.md).
Cubey's purpose here is an experiment/demo: visible payoff should determine
priority. Analytical perfection is not the acceptance criterion for this preview.

**The change is visible in matched dragon images.** Guarded smooth normals read
clearer and more polished in the head grooves and broad neck/body refraction.
The geometric version has a grainier, more crystalline character. The silhouette,
pose, scene and overall transparent-glass identity remain the same. These are
visual observations, not perceptual scores or a universal preference for one
style. They revise the earlier estimate made without a dragon A/B.

The [report evidence](../evidence/raytracing-dragon-glass-normal-looks-2026-11/README.md)
provides a published comparison with a full-scene wipe, individual-mode toggles,
blink and matched head/body/feet crops. Crops are browser viewports into the
original rendered PNGs; no image enhancement or exposure matching is applied.

## Private integration and fidelity

The Q1 helper previously ran only in interface probes. This pass adds a small
integration in two **private source snapshots**, generated by
`projects/raytracing/tools/compare_dragon_normals.py`. They differ only in the
compile-time normal-policy choice. The guard selects the complete interface
normal before the existing sharp dielectric sampler uses it; both reflected
and transmitted radiance paths use that selection. Raw interpolated normals
come from the hit barycentrics and inverse-transpose transform, with the
geometric medium side retained. This is not a new material recipe or ordinary
renderer setting.

Both builds retain geometry, materials, lighting, physical camera, exposure,
sample count, RNG dimensions, spawning, geometric entry/exit, absorption
lengths, straight shadow approximation, footprint model and path budgets.
Their shared foundation-library hashes match. Only `trace.rgen.spv` differs
between the paired shader sets.

At 960 x 540 / 128 SPP, the private geometric build reproduces the ordinary
renderer byte for byte in PNG and raw HDR. At full HD, its geometric PNGs differ
from the retained Q0.1 references by at most one RGB8 code per channel, with
whole-image mean absolute differences below `.000402` code values. Full-HD
byte identity is not claimed. The normal-policy differences are much larger
and can be assessed directly in the report.

## Scope of the visual result

All thirteen captures, including the three pilot controls, have finite,
nonnegative HDR and clean Vulkan synchronization validation. There are no
invalid-medium, query, shadow or stack failures in those captures. The smooth
front/seed-19, side/seed-19 and isolation views each record one cumulative pixel
with an event-budget flag. These are retained in the comparison receipts;
budgets remain 128 events / 1024 radiance / 4096 total queries, cutoff zero.
The other reported views have no positive-weight transport-limit flags.

The Q1 guard's known continuity and energy limitations remain recorded. The
existing zero-curvature footprint approximation also remains under smooth
normals. This pass answers whether the appearance difference is worth judging;
it does not qualify optical accuracy, filtering or a new default.

Blink alternates settled stills. It is not a native motion/popping test or a
temporal-quality result. No native selector, guide/history design, Q2/Q3,
geometry change, commit, push or public-site update is included.

The ordinary source/executable/shaders, accepted assets and separate dirty SDF
worktree are preserved. Future work should be selected from visible demo value
and practical cost; the earlier analytical rejection does not establish that
the current demo's appearance is unacceptable or require another normal-research
loop before exploring other RT effects.
